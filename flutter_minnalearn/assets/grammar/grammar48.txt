1.) Causative verbs (make/let a person do something)

Causative verbs indicate compulsion or permission. They are used when a senior(senpai) person forces or allows a junior person to do something. (Note: If a junior person has a senior person do something, ～て　いただきます is used instead.)

How to make causative verbs:

Group I

Change the last syllable of the ます-form to the corresponding あ-sound and add -せます

Group II

Add -させます to the ます-form

Group III

1.1) N(person)を V(intransitive) causative - make/let a person V(intransitive)

ぶちょうは　ミラーさんを　おおさかへ　しゅちょうさせます。

The department manager makes Mr. Miller go to Osaka on business.

わたしは　むすめを　じゆうに　あそばせました。

I let my daughter play freely.

[Note] When an intransitive verb with "N(place)を" is used in the sentence, the subject of the action is indicated with に instead of を, like so:

わたしは　こどもに　みちの　みぎがわを　あるかせます。

I make my child walk on the right side of the road.

#
1.2) N(person)に Nを V(transitive) causative - make/let a person V(intransitive)

むすめに　あさごはんの　じゅんびを　てつだいわせます。

I make my daughter help prepare breakfast.

せんせいは　せいとに　じゆうに　いけんを　いわせました。

The teacher let her students freely voice their opinions.

2.) V causative て-form いただけませんか　　- Would you please let me do...?

This pattern is used to seek permission.

ねむいですから、そうたいさせて　いただけませんか。

I'm tired, so could you please let me leave early?
