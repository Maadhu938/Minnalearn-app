1) Potential/capable form (can do..)

Potential verbs indicate states, not actions. Thus, although the object of a transitive verb is indicated by the particle を, the object of a potential verb is usually indicated by が:

わたしは　にほんごを　はなします。 I speak Japanese.

わたしは　にほんごが　はなせます。I can speak Japanese.

2) できます

In addition use as "being able to", introduced in Lesson 18, できます can also mean to 'come into being', 'be completed', 'be made', etc:

えきの　まえに　おおきい　スーパーが　できました。　A big supermarket was built in front of the station.

とかいの　しゅうりは　いつ　できますか？　When can you repair the watch by?

#
3) ~しか ~only

しか is appended to nouns, quantifiers, etc., and is always used with a negative. When used with a noun to which a particle is attached, it is appended to the particle unless this is が or を, which it replaces.

ローマじしか　かけません。　I can only write Romaji.

ローマじだけ　かけます。　I can write Romaji (but nothing else).

#
4) Nは　

In addition to introducing a topic, は can also indicate a contrast.

ワインは　のみますが、ビールは　のみません。　I drink wine, but not beer.

#
5) は with another particle already attached

When は is used with a noun to which another particle is attached(other than が or を), it is appended to that particle.

にほんでは　うまを　みる　ことが　できません。　In Japan, you can't see any horses.
